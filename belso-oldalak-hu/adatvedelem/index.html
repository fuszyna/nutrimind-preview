10. Your rights in connection with data processing (data subject rights)

You have the following rights in connection with the processing of your personal data:

a) Right of access (Article 15 GDPR): You are entitled to obtain information from the Service Provider as to whether your personal data are being processed and, where that is the case, you are entitled to access the personal data and the information contained in this notice.

Upon request, the Service Provider will provide you with a copy of the personal data undergoing processing. For any further copies requested by you, the Service Provider may charge a reasonable fee based on administrative costs. Where you have submitted the request by electronic means, we will provide the information in a commonly used electronic form, unless you request otherwise.

b) Right to rectification (Article 16 GDPR): You are entitled to have the Service Provider, at your request, rectify inaccurate personal data concerning you without undue delay. You are also entitled to request that incomplete personal data be completed.

c) Right to erasure (Article 17 GDPR): You are entitled to have the Service Provider, at your request, erase personal data concerning you without delay, and the Service Provider is obliged to erase personal data concerning you without delay where one of the following grounds applies:

  • the personal data are no longer necessary in relation to the purposes for which they were collected or otherwise processed;
  • the legal basis for the processing is your consent, you withdraw it, and there is no other legal ground for the processing;
  • you object to the processing and there are no overriding legitimate grounds for the processing;
  • the personal data have been unlawfully processed;
  • the personal data have to be erased for compliance with a legal obligation under Union or Member State law to which the Service Provider is subject.

The above provisions of this point c) do not apply to the extent that processing is necessary (i) for compliance with an obligation under Union or Member State law to which the Service Provider is subject and which requires the processing of personal data, or (ii) for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims.

d) Right to restriction of processing (Article 18 GDPR): You are entitled to have the Service Provider, at your request, restrict processing where one of the following applies:

  • you contest the accuracy of the personal data, in which case the restriction applies for a period enabling the Service Provider to verify the accuracy of the personal data;
  • the processing is unlawful and you oppose the erasure of the data and request the restriction of their use instead;
  • the Service Provider no longer needs the personal data for the purposes of the processing, but you require them for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims; or
  • you have objected to the processing; in this case the restriction applies pending the verification of whether the legitimate grounds of the Service Provider override your legitimate grounds.

e) Right to object (Article 21 GDPR): You are entitled to object at any time, on grounds relating to your particular situation, to the processing of your personal data. In this case, the Service Provider may no longer process the personal data unless the Service Provider demonstrates compelling legitimate grounds for the processing which override your interests, rights and freedoms, or which relate to the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims. Where the processing takes place for compliance with a legal obligation, the exercise of the right to object does not result in the cessation of the processing under this notice.

f) Right to information concerning the above rights (Article 12 GDPR): The Service Provider will inform you of the circumstances of the data processing in a concise, transparent, intelligible and clear manner without delay, and in any event within one month of receipt of your request under points a)–e) above. Where necessary, taking into account the complexity and number of the requests, this period may be extended by a further two months. The Service Provider will inform you of any such extension, together with the reasons for the delay, within one month of receipt of the request.

The information is provided free of charge. Where your request is manifestly unfounded or excessive, in particular because of its repetitive character, the Service Provider may, taking into account the administrative costs of providing the information or communication or taking the action requested: (i) charge a reasonable fee, or (ii) refuse to act on the request. The burden of demonstrating the manifestly unfounded or excessive character of the request lies with the Service Provider.

The Service Provider will communicate the matters set out in points b)–d) above (i.e. any rectification, erasure or restriction of processing) to each recipient to whom the personal data have been disclosed, unless this proves impossible or involves disproportionate effort. The Company will inform you about those recipients if you request it.

g) Right to lodge a complaint (Article 77 GDPR): You are entitled to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority – in particular in the Member State of your habitual residence, place of work or place of the alleged infringement – if you consider that the processing of personal data relating to you infringes the GDPR. A complaint may be lodged with the Nemzeti Adatvédelmi és Információszabadság Hatóság (NAIH) (address: 1055 Budapest, Falk Miksa utca 9-11.; telephone: +36 1 391 1400; naih.hu; ugyfelszolgalat@naih.hu).

h) Right to an effective judicial remedy (Article 79 GDPR): You are entitled to an effective judicial remedy if you consider that your rights under the GDPR have been infringed as a result of the processing of your personal data in non-compliance with the GDPR. Proceedings against the Service Provider must be brought before the courts of the Member State in which the Service Provider has its establishment. Such proceedings may also be brought before the courts of the Member State of your habitual residence.

i) Right to data portability (Article 20 GDPR): You have the right to data portability where the legal basis for the processing is Article 6(1)(a) or (b) of the GDPR, i.e. your consent or the performance of a contract concluded with you. In this case, you are entitled to receive the personal data concerning you, which you have provided to the Service Provider, in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format, and you are entitled to transmit those data to another controller without hindrance from the Service Provider. You are also entitled to request, where technically feasible, that the Service Provider transmit the personal data directly to another controller.
